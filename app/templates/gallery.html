Color coding scheme of GUIDANCE



Vpu is an accessory viral protein present in human immunodeficiency virus 1 (HIV-1) and simian immunodeficiency viruses (SIVs) infecting chimpanzees and other primate species, yet absent in HIV-2. The protein contains ~80 amino-acids and has two known major functions, which are conducted by two distinct domains of the protein: promotion of CD4 degradation via the cytoplasmic domain, and enhancement of virion release from host cells via the trans-membrane domain [1]. The latter activity has shown to be related to antagonism of Tetherin, an innate immune protein that blocks the release of nascent virus from infected cells in the absence of Vpu[2].

In order to exemplify the power and ease of GUIDANCE, the web-server was run on a sample of Vpu protein sequences from the three main HIV-1 groups (M, N and O) and SIV sequences from chimpanzee (Pan troglodytes), gorilla (Gorilla gorilla), and several Cercopithecus species, using the MAFFT method. The results clearly show that the alignment of the cytoplasmic domain of Vpu is not robust to perturbations in the guide tree. The same applies to some residues in the transmembrane and extracellular domains (Figure 1a). Looking at specific sequences, the SIV sequences from Cercopithecus and some of the sequences from P. troglodytes are shown to be badly aligned with the rest of the sequence set.

Note that if those sequences are removed, the trans-membrane domain will become a confidently aligned block (Figure 1b). Subsequent analyses based on this MSA should take the alignment uncertainty into account. For example, if our goal is positive selection inference (see the Selecton server) then predictions regarding unreliable regions such as the first or the last dozen of columns should be taken with a grain of salt.

Phylogeny across alternative MSAs (new in GUIDANCE3)


GUIDANCE3 also uses the alternative MSAs for tree inference. It infers a maximum-likelihood tree from each of 20 randomly chosen alternative MSAs and summarizes them in the GUIDANCE3 tree, an extended majority-rule consensus resolved on the base MSA. Each branch is coloured by its alignment support: the percentage of the 20 trees that contain it. A branch with low alignment support depends on how the sequences were aligned, even when standard support measures computed on a single MSA are high.

The example below is the result for the 14 HIV-1 group M Vpu sequences (subtypes A1, B, C, D, F1 and G) of the "Load an example" button on the home page, run with default parameters. The subtype C and A1 clades are robust to alignment uncertainty (100% alignment support, aBayes ≈ 1). In contrast, the deeper branches that group subtypes B, D and G appear in only 45–55% of the trees, and one branch within subtype C has SH-aLRT 67 on the base MSA but only 40% alignment support. Hover over a branch to see its alignment support, aBayes and SH-aLRT.

Is the GUIDANCE3 tree more accurate? A mammalian example with a known tree


To check a tree against the truth, the true tree must be known. In this example, protein sequences were simulated along the tree of a mammalian gene from the OrthoMaM database (40 species; substitution and indel parameters estimated from the real gene), so the true tree is known exactly. GUIDANCE3 was run on the unaligned simulated sequences with default parameters (MAFFT). The figure compares (a) the true tree with (b) the maximum-likelihood tree inferred from the default MAFFT alignment and (c) the GUIDANCE3 tree. Branches that are not in the true tree are dashed and marked with a cross; all trees are rooted on the echidna, as in OrthoMaM.

The tree from the default MSA has 13 wrong branches out of 37, the GUIDANCE3 tree 6 (an ML tree inferred from the true alignment, which is never known in practice, has 7). Alignment support also flags the remaining doubtful branches: most wrong branches have low alignment support (in the default tree, 11 of the 13 are supported by 30% of the alternative-MSA trees or fewer), while its correct branches have a median alignment support of 80%.

The 20 alternative MSAs are chosen at random, so results vary slightly between runs: with five different random sets of 20, the GUIDANCE3 tree had 6–9 wrong branches (always fewer than the 13 of the default tree). With 100 alternative MSAs (an option under Advanced Options), every random set gave 6.

This is one illustrative dataset. Over 340 simulated datasets based on OrthoMaM genes, the GUIDANCE3 tree was closer to the true tree than the tree from the default MAFFT alignment in 259 datasets, equal in 15 and further in 66 (mean normalized Robinson–Foulds distance 0.328 vs 0.363).
